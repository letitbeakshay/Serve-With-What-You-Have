The clothes are ready. The knowing is the hard part.

When we ask people why the bag is still in the cupboard, almost nobody says they did not want to give it away. They say they were not sure anyone would want it.

Would a home take used clothes, or only new ones? Would they mind that two shirts have a small mark? Is it worth driving over with three bags and being told, politely, that they cannot use any of it?

So we ask first. Homes and shelters tell us what they are short of and what sizes they need. When you tell us what you have, we already know who has asked for it.

Wash it if you can. Send it anyway if you cannot.

A washed bag saves us a step, and we would always rather have one. But we know how it goes. The bag is packed, the week gets away from you, and it sits by the door for another month.

So do not let the washing be the reason it stays in the house. Every bag that reaches us is washed properly at our end before it goes anywhere. Send it as it is and we will take it from there.

The only thing we ask is that nothing goes in wet, because damp cloth spoils whatever is packed beside it.

Nothing worn out ever reaches a home.

Homes are often sent bags of whatever people wanted rid of, and then spend a day working out what is usable and disposing of the rest. It is a cost passed to the people who can least afford it.

We do not do that. Anything too worn to be given is kept out and sent for recycling, where it becomes fresh cloth rather than landfill. What arrives at a home is washed, sorted and wearable.

We also check who a home actually looks after before sending anything. A home for elderly residents does not need school uniforms. Matching properly takes us longer, and it is the part that makes this worth doing at all.

Where your clothes actually end up.

We only work with places we have spoken to and visited. Every one of them tells us what they need and who they look after before anything is sent.

Children's homes

School age cotton runs out fastest here. Shirts, shorts, frocks and uniforms in sizes four to fourteen.

Old age homes

Bedsheets, towels and simple daily wear. Soft cotton and easy fastenings matter more than size here.

Night shelters

Warm layers, blankets and footwear, mostly for men. Hard wearing items matter far more here than anything smart.

Community schools

Uniforms, plain white shirts and school bags, usually needed in the weeks before a new term.

Families nearby

Where a home tells us of a household close by that is short of something specific.

And the rest, to recycling

Anything too worn to wear goes to a textile recycler and comes back as new cloth. It never reaches a home as someone else's problem to throw away.

Questions people ask before
they send the first message.

Where do I take the clothes?

To a collection point near you. Once you message us we will tell you the closest one and the hours it is open. There are only a few for now, so if none is convenient, say so and we will look at other options with you.

Can you collect from my house?

Sometimes. We do not have a regular pickup service yet, but we do arrange it on request when we can, especially for larger quantities or if you have no way to travel. Ask us and we will tell you honestly whether we can manage it.

Do I need to wash everything first?

Only if you can. We wash every piece properly ourselves before it goes anywhere, so an unwashed bag is still welcome. Just make sure nothing is packed wet, because damp cloth ruins whatever is beside it.

What happens to clothes that are too worn to give?

They are set aside and sent for recycling, where the fabric is turned back into new cloth. They are never passed on to a home. Homes should not be left doing our sorting for us.

How do you decide which home gets what?

Homes tell us what they are short of and who they look after. We match against that item by item, not bag by bag, so what you send is usually divided between a few places. We do not send a bag to a home simply because we have a bag.

Will I know where my clothes went?

Yes. You will get a message when your bag has been sorted, and another once it has been delivered. One bag often goes to more than one place, since we divide it by what each home has asked for, and we will tell you where each part of it went.

How much do I need to have?

There is no minimum. One bag is worth a message. If it is a single shirt, hold on to it until you have a little more, so the trip is worth your time.

Is there any cost, now or later?

None. This is free and we do not collect money at any point. If anyone asks you to pay something in our name, please tell us.

Can I give money instead?

Not through us. We do not take payments of any kind. If you want to give money, take it directly to a home you have chosen.
